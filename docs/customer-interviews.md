Market Validation: Listening to the Coaches
To determine whether CoachIQ addresses a meaningful business problem, our team conducted customer interviews with three Calgary-area baseball coaches: Luke Snefft, Brett Potter and Ethan Webster.
Their collective coaching experience spans youth development programs, camps, high school athletics, university-level baseball and collegiate summer leagues. This provided our team with perspectives from several of the competitive levels CoachIQ is designed to serve.
Practice Planning for Real-World Conditions
A consistent challenge identified across all three interviews was the complexity of organizing effective practices.
The coaches estimated that approximately half of their practice time in Calgary takes place indoors, where limited space, equipment availability and fluctuating player attendance create additional planning challenges.
They expressed interest in a practice planning tool capable of accounting for these constraints while recommending player-specific drills and structured training sessions targeting team weaknesses.
A recurring theme throughout the interviews was the opportunity cost of administrative work. Time spent organizing practices is time that could otherwise be dedicated to athlete development, instruction and direct player engagement.
This feedback reinforced the value of CoachIQ's practice planner, which translates statistical weaknesses into structured training recommendations. It also identified opportunities for future development, particularly incorporating facility constraints, equipment availability and player attendance into practice generation.
Data-Driven Bullpen Management
Ethan Webster, who has experience as both a pitcher and pitching coach, identified particular value in CoachIQ's bullpen management functionality.
Access to information about pitcher availability, recent workload and estimated fatigue could support more informed decisions about pitching assignments. According to Webster, this level of analytical support is not readily available to many teams.
Beyond competitive performance, he emphasized the importance of positioning athletes for success while considering their health and safety.
This feedback highlighted the potential for CoachIQ to support responsible workload management and long-term player development, while recognizing that analytical recommendations should complement rather than replace a coach's judgment.
From Customer Insights to Business Value
The consistency of the feedback across three independent interviews provided encouraging preliminary evidence that CoachIQ addresses relevant coaching challenges.
Calgary's climate creates a particularly compelling use case for adaptable practice planning, while the demand for accessible performance analytics highlights an opportunity to support teams without dedicated analytical staff.
Although these interviews represent a relatively small sample, they helped establish a clearer direction for our product and reinforced its central value proposition:
CoachIQ simplifies planning and performance analysis so coaches can spend more time doing what matters most — coaching.
